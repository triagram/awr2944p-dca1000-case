# Why v3.1 changed what it changed

English | [简体中文](v3.1-changes.zh-CN.md)

Design decisions of 2026-10-23. The outer dimensions, the parts and the way the case goes together
are the same as in v3.0. The body, the front guard and the desk foot changed; the knob and the spacer
did not. In TI's 3 dB and 6 dB beams nothing new blocks or reflects the signal, as in v3.0
(see [radar-assessment.md](radar-assessment.md)).

## 1. Stiffer wall piece at the J8 notch

The upper half of the notch for the radar's J8 USB port is closed into a window, and the inside corner
of the vertical drop gets an R3 fillet. The piece of wall that carries the lower guard clip is joined
to the rest of the wall again: under a 10 N side push the stress between layers falls from about
34 MPa to about 10 MPa, and the deflection from 4.7 mm to 2.6 mm.

The J8 plug (checked as an 11 × 7.5 mm body) goes through the 15 × 11 mm window without touching; the
top of the window lines up with the top of the J12 window. The paths along which the radar board and
the DCA1000 go in from the front are clear. The one consequence is that the J8 USB cable now has to be
plugged in from the side after the radar board is in.

**Are there other pieces like it?** No. Every thin part attached on one side only that a clip or a
screw pulls on was checked, on the body and on the guard. Only the J8 piece was attached at one end
and carried a clip at the other; the rest are attached at both ends, short and thick, or carry no
load. With the same plate model, the wall piece of the upper clip, attached at the corner of the top
wall, sees 3 MPa and 0.08 mm under a 10 N side push, and the strip above the RJ45 opening, attached at
both ends, at most about 10 MPa.

## 2. Rounded corners and chamfers

1. The two sharp corners at the open right side of the guard's antenna window: R2.5.
2. The front edge of the guard and of the LED window: 0.6 mm chamfer; the back edge of the body:
   0.8 mm chamfer. An edge on the print bed can only be chamfered, and the chamfer also offsets the
   first-layer elephant's foot.
3. The lowered section of the right wall beside the antenna: R0.8 on its top edge, R3 at its inside
   corner.
4. The knife edge of the bottom rail lip is cut back to a 0.4 mm flat.

The top corner of the vertical drop is not rounded: it sits under the guard where nobody touches it,
and rounding it would add a small slope that reflects oblique signal forward.

## 3. The bottom rail runs into both corners

The rail lip now reaches both corners and is trimmed to the R4 outline of the case; the lower corners
of the guard follow the same outline. The way the guard goes on is unchanged: the whole movement
(45 positions) was checked for collisions, and when pulled outward the guard locks after 0.25 to
0.4 mm, as before. The lip now holds the lower edge of the guard over the full 137.8 mm instead of
129 mm.

## 4. Lighter parts

The honeycomb grid in the back does not change the strength of the case but is mostly for looks and
air flow, and saves 2 g. Under the desk foot small cells that do not go through make the part heavier;
only 20 mm cells with a 1 mm top skin save weight, about 5 g.

**Back grid.** A plate model of the back compares the slots of v3.0 with a honeycomb grid (6 mm cells,
1.8 mm ribs). The back is printed flat on the bed, so bending stress runs along the layers; Bambu PLA
Basic has an in-layer bending strength of 76 MPa.

| Load | Slots (v3.0) | Honeycomb (v3.1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boards pressing on the 4 posts, 20 N | 0.16 mm, 5 MPa | 0.20 mm, 5 MPa |
| A finger pressing the middle of the grid, 2 kg (20 N) | 0.55 mm, 10 MPa | 0.92 mm, 17 MPa |

Under normal loads there is almost no difference. Pressed by hand in the middle it is a little softer,
at less than a quarter of the strength. The grid has twice the vent area of the slots.

**Desk foot.** A printed solid plate already has 15 % sparse infill inside; its weight is in the top
and bottom skins and the outer walls. Pockets that do not go through add walls and floors, so 10 mm
cells cut half deep make the foot 3 g heavier. Only large, deep cells pay off.

| Desk foot | Estimated weight | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid (v3.0) | 49.4 g | |
| 10 mm honeycomb, half deep (2.5 mm) | 52.6 g | +3.2 g |
| 10 mm honeycomb, 0.8 mm top skin | 49.8 g | +0.4 g |
| 20 mm honeycomb, 1 mm top skin (chosen) | 44.4 g | −5.0 g |
| 4 large blind pockets, 1 mm top skin | 43.5 g | −5.9 g |
| 4 through windows (rejected) | 35.8 g | −13.6 g |

The 20 mm honeycomb is on the underside (facing up when printed, so no supports) and the top surface
is unchanged. The screw holes, the tripod hole and the rubber foot spots stay solid. The foot only
stands on a desk, so a 1 mm skin with 2.4 mm ribs is enough.

**Top vents.** The slots in the top wall now run to the right side (−1 g), with no effect on strength.

The weights are estimated the way a slicer counts them (0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 15 % infill); the
numbers Bambu Studio shows can differ by about 10 %.

## 5. Keep the print pause

The closed nut pockets stay: a pause at 47.00 mm (layer 235) seals the knob nuts in, and they can never
fall out. The body file name carries the pause height so the pause is not forgotten. The alternative,
a slot to push the nut in from the side, needs no pause, but the nut could fall out whenever a knob is
unscrewed.
